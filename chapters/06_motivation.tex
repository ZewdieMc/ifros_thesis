\textbf{Dear SWEET Selection Committee}

\paragraph{\noindent
\textnormal{
I am writing to express my enthusiastic interest in the interdisciplinary PhD opportunities within the SWEET consortium, particularly the research themes of 1) Situation Awareness and Complex Scene Understanding, 2) Navigation Styles for Guiding Robots, and 3) Adaptive Robot Decision-Making for Service Robots. As a current Erasmus Mundus Joint Master’s student in Intelligent Field Robotic Systems (anticipated graduation: June 2025), I am honing my expertise in robotic perception, navigation, and decision-making through advanced coursework and thesis research. My hands-on experience in SLAM, machine learning, and autonomous systems with SWEET’s visionary focus on safety, adaptability, and social intelligence—positions me to contribute meaningfully to these projects.}}

\paragraph{\noindent
\textnormal{
For my ongoing Master’s thesis, I am developing a LiDAR-based SLAM system using Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for odometry estimation and pose graph optimization for mapping. This work emphasizes robust localization and mapping in unstructured environments, directly aligning with the foundational needs of domestic scene understanding. My prior project on Pose-Based EKF SLAM using NDT for point cloud registration further demonstrates my ability to integrate sensor data (LiDAR, IMU, wheel encoders) for precise mapping in dynamic scenarios. Additionally, I designed a CNN-based object detection system to classify and localize household items (e.g., chairs, tables) in RGB-D data, achieving 98\% accuracy on a smaller dataset. This performance was competitive with YOLOv8, which scored 99\% on the same dataset. Integrating such semantic capabilities with SLAM could bridge geometric and contextual understanding—critical for proactive risk detection (e.g., identifying unattended hazards near vulnerable users).}}

\paragraph{\textnormal{
My project on Autonomous Frontier-Based Robot Exploration and Path Planning required developing an exploration system integrating Frontier-Based Exploration, RRT* for global path planning, and DWA for local navigation. This system prioritized high-potential exploration targets while ensuring smooth, collision-free navigation—skills directly transferable to designing navigation styles that guide humans effectively. Additionally, my work on Camera Pose Estimation using Feature Matching (using SIFT, FLANN, and RANSAC) honed my ability to model spatial relationships, which could inform how robots signal intent through motion (e.g., adjusting trajectories to communicate caution).
}}

\paragraph{
\textnormal{
While my thesis focuses on LiDAR-based SLAM using EKF for odometry estimation and pose graph optimization, I have gained relevant experience in decision-making through other projects. For example, in my Autonomous Frontier-Based Robot Exploration and Path Planning project, I developed algorithms to balance exploration efficiency with safety constraints, ensuring the robot could navigate unknown environments effectively. This experience aligns with the challenge of reconciling user and bystander preferences in service robotics. I am particularly interested in exploring Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) to model human preferences and adapt robot behavior accordingly. Additionally, my work on object detection using CNNs and camera pose estimation has given me practical experience with machine learning and computer vision, which I aim to apply to adaptive decision-making frameworks.
